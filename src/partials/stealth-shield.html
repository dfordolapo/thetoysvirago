Plate tectonics

Plate tectonics (from the Late Latin tectonicus, from Ancient Greek τεκτονικός 'pertaining to building') is the generally accepted scientific theory that Earth's outer rigid lithosphere is broken into several large and small tectonic plates that move slowly over the mechanically weaker asthenosphere below.

The model builds on the concept of continental drift, an idea developed during the first decades of the 20th century. The geoscientific community accepted plate-tectonic theory after seafloor spreading was validated in the late 1950s and early 1960s.

Key principles

The outer layers of Earth are divided into the lithosphere and the asthenosphere. The division is based on mechanical properties rather than chemical composition alone. The lithosphere is cooler and more rigid, comprising the crust and the uppermost part of the mantle.

Types of plate boundaries

Three primary types of plate boundaries exist along tectonic margins:

  • Divergent boundaries: where two plates slide apart from each other (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge).
  • Convergent boundaries: where two plates slide toward each other, forming either a subduction zone or continental collision (e.g., Himalayas, Andes).
  • Transform boundaries: where plates slide past one another horizontally along fault lines (e.g., San Andreas Fault).

CBN Issues Updated Liquidity Risk Guidelines Ahead of Q1 MPC Policy Review

Commercial lenders required to maintain enhanced high-quality liquid asset buffers to bolster interbank settlement resilience amid ongoing macroeconomic stabilization.

By Economics & Banking Bureau Published Oct 27, 2026 · 4 min read
Share: X · LinkedIn · Email

ABUJA - The Central Bank of Nigeria has circulated revised liquidity risk guidelines to all licensed deposit money banks and discount houses, reinforcing statutory capital adequacy ratios and establishing stricter supervisory surveillance on short-term interbank exposures.

According to the regulatory circular signed by the Director of Financial Policy and Regulation, tier-1 institutions must sustain an adjusted Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) designed to withstand 30-day liquidity stress scenarios across varying foreign exchange inflow cycles.

"The objective is to consolidate the stability of the domestic financial architecture while deepening private sector credit transmission across manufacturing, clean energy, and agricultural export value chains," the document stated.

Analysts at Lagos-based investment houses noted that the directive reflects sustained coordination between monetary authorities and fiscal debt managers, aiming to stabilize sovereign bond yields and anchor inflationary expectations following the third-quarter monetary policy committee resolutions.

Treasury desks across major commercial banks indicated that liquidity conditions remained orderly, with interbank repo rates trading within the asymmetric corridor around the Monetary Policy Rate (MPR).

Authentic Smoky Nigerian Party Jollof Rice (The Secret to That Lagos Party Flavor)

PREP TIME20 Mins
COOK TIME50 Mins
TOTAL TIME1 Hr 10 Mins
SERVINGS8 People
RATING★ 4.9 (380+ reviews)

There is a distinct aroma and smoky bottom-pan taste that makes Nigerian party jollof rice unforgettable. The secret isn't firewood alone: it's the specific ratio of red bell peppers (tatashe) to tomatoes, a heavily reduced pepper paste, and letting the rice steam tightly under foil until it catches slightly on the base of the cast iron pot.

Ingredients Checklist

For The Jollof Pepper Base:

  • 6 large Red Bell Peppers (Tatashe), deseeded
  • 4 Scotch Bonnet Peppers (Atarodo)
  • 5 medium Plum Tomatoes
  • 2 medium Red Onions
  • 1 thumb-sized fresh Ginger & 3 Garlic cloves
  • 100g double concentrated Tomato Paste

For The Rice & Aromatics:

  • 4 cups Long grain parboiled rice (washed thoroughly)
  • 2.5 cups rich beef or chicken bone broth
  • 1 cup vegetable cooking oil
  • 1 tbsp dried Thyme & 1 tbsp Curry powder
  • 4 whole dried Bay leaves
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter (for finishing sheen)
  • 1 medium onion (sliced for frying base)

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Blend & Boil the Pepper Base: Blend the tatashe, atarodo, plum tomatoes, onions, ginger, and garlic into a smooth puree. Pour into a saucepan and boil over medium-high heat until the water evaporates and you are left with a thick paste.
  2. Fry the Base: In a heavy-bottomed pot, heat the vegetable oil and fry sliced onions until fragrant and lightly caramelized. Add the tomato paste and fry for 5 minutes until it darkens slightly. Pour in the boiled pepper puree and fry for 15 minutes, stirring continuously until oil floats to the top.
  3. Season & Add Stock: Add thyme, curry powder, bay leaves, seasoning cubes, and salt. Pour in your hot meat stock and bring to a rolling boil. Taste and adjust seasoning: it should taste slightly saltier than usual because the rice will absorb it.
  4. Add Rice & Seal: Stir in the thoroughly washed and drained parboiled rice. The liquid should sit just level with the top of the rice. Cover the pot with heavy-duty aluminum foil to trap every drop of steam, then clamp the pot lid down firmly.
  5. Low Heat Steam: Turn the heat down to low. Let the rice steam undisturbed for 30 minutes. Uncover, gently turn with a wooden spoon or spatula from the bottom up, place butter slices on top, reseal with foil, and let it cook for another 10 to 15 minutes.
  6. The Signature Party Burn: For that authentic smoky aroma, increase heat to medium-low for the final 5 minutes until you hear a gentle crackle and smell that distinct rich smokiness. Turn off heat and let it rest for 10 minutes before serving.
